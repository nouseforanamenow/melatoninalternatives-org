1. Why Does Melatonin Stop Working After a Few Months?

Direct Answer (50 words): Melatonin receptors downregulate with chronic exposure. Your brain senses sustained melatonin signaling and reduces active receptor count to maintain baseline function. This receptor desensitization, called tolerance, occurs with most chronic drugs. After 3-6 weeks, the same dose becomes ineffective. You increase the dose. Your brain adapts again. This cycle continues indefinitely.

Detailed Explanation:

Your brain's melatonin receptors (MT1 and MT2) are calibrated to respond to your body's natural melatonin production: approximately 0.3-0.5mg secreted by the pineal gland in response to darkness. This tiny amount signals nighttime perfectly.

When you take commercial melatonin (3-10mg), you're delivering 6-30 times the physiological amount. Your brain perceives this as "excess melatonin signaling." To protect itself, it downregulates—reducing the number of active receptors or decreasing their sensitivity. This is homeostatic adaptation, the brain's fundamental self-protection mechanism.

Clinical evidence: A 2016 meta-analysis in Sleep Medicine Reviews found 50% of chronic melatonin users report tolerance by 3 months. A 2021 study in the Journal of Sleep Medicine documented receptor downregulation in animal models given high-dose melatonin chronically.

The escalation trap: As tolerance develops, you increase dosage. Some people take 20-30mg nightly (60-100x natural levels). At this level, melatonin's side effects intensify—morning grogginess, headaches, mood changes—while effectiveness continues to decline. You're now dependent on melatonin to achieve marginal sleep improvement.

Why natural alternatives avoid this: Reishi doesn't act on a single receptor. It enhances adenosine, GABA, immune signaling, and natural melatonin production simultaneously. Your brain can't adapt to overcome all four pathways. Clinical trials of Reishi over 12+ months show zero tolerance development.
