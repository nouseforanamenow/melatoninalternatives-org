I Quit Melatonin for 30 Days—Here's What Happened

Published February 2025 · 10 min read

After two years of nightly melatonin use, I was taking 10mg and still waking up groggy. Here's my week-by-week account of transitioning to reishi mushroom—the rough nights, the recovery, and why I'm never going back.

I started taking melatonin in 2022. 3mg at first, and it worked beautifully. I fell asleep within 20 minutes, slept through the night, and woke up refreshed.

Fast forward two years: I was taking 10mg, falling asleep okay but waking up at 3am, and dragging myself through mornings with a fog that didn't lift until noon. Classic melatonin tolerance.

I knew something had to change. After researching alternatives, I decided to quit melatonin cold turkey and switch to reishi mushroom. This is what happened.

The Transition Protocol

Based on my research, I didn't want to quit cold turkey and have my sleep completely fall apart. I used this approach:

My Melatonin Transition Protocol

  1. Week 1: Cut melatonin to 5mg. Start reishi (Ahara Elixir, 2ml) 30 minutes before bed.
  2. Week 2: Cut melatonin to 2.5mg. Continue reishi at same dose.
  3. Week 3: Drop melatonin entirely. Continue reishi.
  4. Week 4+: Reishi only. Monitor and adjust.

I chose Ahara Reishi Elixir specifically because it's the only product I found that verifies adenosine content—the compound actually responsible for reishi's sleep benefits. Most reishi products don't test for it.

Week 1: The Taper Begins

Days 1-7: Melatonin 5mg + Reishi

Sleep quality: Actually slightly better. Cutting the melatonin dose in half didn't hurt as much as I expected.

Morning grogginess: Noticeably reduced. This was the first sign that my high melatonin dose was part of the problem.

Notable: The reishi has a mild, slightly bitter taste. I started mixing it into chamomile tea which masked it completely.

I was surprised how well the first week went. I'd been scared to reduce my melatonin dose, but 5mg + reishi actually worked better than 10mg alone. The morning fog lifted faster.

Week 2: Cutting Deeper

Days 8-14: Melatonin 2.5mg + Reishi

Sleep quality: Mixed. Nights 8-10 were rough—took longer to fall asleep, woke up more. Nights 11-14 stabilized.

Morning grogginess: Almost gone. Even on rough nights, mornings were clearer than my baseline.

Notable: Day 9 was the worst night. Took 90 minutes to fall asleep. I almost gave up and took more melatonin, but stuck with it.

Week 2 was the hardest. My body was clearly adjusting to less external melatonin. I expected this—the research says melatonin receptor upregulation takes time. The fact that mornings were still better kept me going.

Week 3: Going Zero

Days 15-21: Reishi Only

Sleep quality: Rocky start, then improvement. Night 15-16 were difficult. By night 19, I was sleeping better than I had in months.

Morning grogginess: None. First time in two years I woke up without that melatonin hangover.

Notable: I started dreaming vividly again. Hadn't realized melatonin had been suppressing my REM sleep.

Dropping the last 2.5mg was psychologically harder than physically. Nights 15-16, I lay awake thinking, "This isn't working. I need my melatonin back." But I'd committed to the experiment.

By night 19, something shifted. I fell asleep in under 30 minutes, slept through until 6am, and woke up before my alarm feeling actually rested. No fog. No grogginess. Clear.

Week 4: The New Normal

Days 22-30: Reishi Only (Stabilized)

Sleep quality: Consistent. 25-35 minutes to fall asleep, 0-1 night wakings, 7-8 hours total.

Morning grogginess: Zero. Waking up refreshed became the norm, not the exception.

Notable: My afternoon energy also improved. Didn't realize how much the melatonin hangover was affecting my whole day.

What I Learned

The tolerance was real

I'd been telling myself the melatonin was still helping. It wasn't. My body had adapted to it, and I was just feeding a dependency without benefit.

The grogginess was from the melatonin, not poor sleep

I assumed I felt groggy because I wasn't sleeping well. Turns out, the melatonin itself was causing the grogginess. Even on nights when I slept poorly during the transition, mornings were clearer.

The transition period is real but manageable

Nights 8-10 and 15-16 were rough. But it was 6 bad nights out of 30, and I knew they were temporary. If you go in expecting some difficulty, it's easier to push through.

Reishi works differently—and better for me

Melatonin felt like being knocked out. Reishi feels like naturally getting tired. It's a subtle difference, but the sleep quality is noticeably better. I dream more, wake up more easily, and feel more rested.

Important Caveat

This is my personal experience, not medical advice. I was a healthy adult with no underlying sleep disorders. If you have a diagnosed sleep condition, work with your doctor on any supplement changes.

Would I Do It Again?

Absolutely. I wish I'd done it sooner.

The melatonin tolerance had been building for months, and I'd been in denial about it. Every few months I'd increase my dose, telling myself "this is the dose that will work." It was a losing game.

Reishi, by contrast, has been consistent. I've been using it for 4 months now at the same dose, with the same results. No tolerance. No need to increase. No grogginess.

Tips If You're Considering This

Don't do it during a stressful period. The transition nights are harder when you're already anxious. Pick a relatively calm few weeks.

Taper, don't quit cold turkey. The gradual reduction made this manageable. Cold turkey would have been miserable.

Start the alternative before dropping melatonin. Having reishi on board before I reduced melatonin gave my body something to work with.

Track your sleep. It's easy to catastrophize during rough nights. Having objective data ("okay, I slept 6 hours, that's not great but not terrible") helps.

Commit to the full 30 days. The first two weeks aren't representative. Your body needs time to readjust. Don't judge the results until week 4.
